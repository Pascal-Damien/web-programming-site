Origins

In the 1960s, researchers developed ways for computers to communicate across networks. In 1969, the Advanced Research Projects Agency Network (ARPANET) connected its first four nodes and became an important foundation for the Internet.

Important Milestones

  1. 1969: ARPANET sends its first message.
  2. 1971: Ray Tomlinson develops network email.
  3. 1983: ARPANET adopts TCP/IP, the core communication protocol of the Internet.
  4. 1989: Tim Berners-Lee proposes the World Wide Web.
  5. 1991: The first website becomes available to the public.
  6. 1993: The Mosaic browser helps make the Web easier to use.

The World Wide Web

The World Wide Web is a system of linked pages and resources accessed through the Internet. It uses technologies such as HTML, HTTP, and URLs. The Web's simple, open design helped the Internet become accessible to people around the world.

The Modern Internet

Since the 2000s, broadband, smartphones, cloud computing, social media, and streaming services have transformed how people communicate, learn, work, and share information online. Today, billions of people and devices connect through the Internet.
